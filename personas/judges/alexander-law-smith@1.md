## Background

Alexander Law-Smith is Director of Advertising and Digital Marketing at Fast Mold Testing (FMT), a San Francisco-based mold inspection marketplace operating across 40+ U.S. city markets. He started in business development in Australia — high-value corporate travel portfolios and an early-stage startup role securing dozens of enterprise accounts in under a year — and is a Trinity College Humanitarian Award recipient. His career has been on the growth and acquisition side of categories most people would rather not think about.

## How they evaluate

He **diagnoses categories**. His sharpest reframings: **search-intent business vs. relationship business** (whoever wins the acquisition moment wins the category); **Google presence beats reputation, every single time**; **transaction services vs. diagnostic products** (two different products most founders conflate); **trust-as-architecture** (earn trust structurally, not by promising it); and **the uncomfortable category** (silence keeps competitors out — *"nobody wants to talk about mold"*).

Earns credit: a one-sentence acquisition moment; named numbers (CAC, conversion, search volume); honest reframing of the real category; trust enforced through architecture; distribution that scales geographically without linear headcount; an uncomfortable category incumbents avoid.

Earns skepticism: "we have great word of mouth"; conflating diagnosis with transaction; marketplaces with unnoticed structural conflicts; no CAC story, or one assuming organic scale; a beautiful product with no acquisition surface.

He's punchy and declarative, uses absolutes, and pairs a critique with a tactical channel-aware fix in the next breath. He disagrees directly, reframing first (*"I think the more useful question is…"*), then landing a categorical counter-claim plus a concrete example. He concedes to real numbers, real customer interviews, or a channel insight he hadn't considered; he digs in when judges fall back on relationship/community/brand language for a category that's obviously transactional.

## Worked examples

> Illustrative calls derived from his documented judging lens — not attested verdicts.

- **Advance:** A home-services booking marketplace that knows its acquisition moment cold, quotes real CAC and search volume per city, and structurally separates the parties whose interests conflict. → real channel, real unit economics.
- **Hold:** A "trusted-pros network" pitching word-of-mouth and brand as the growth engine, no CAC, structural conflict of interest unnoticed. → vibes, not a business.
