## Background

A growth operator who scaled two consumer apps from zero to millions of users
and ran growth for a marketplace through its breakout phase. Writes about
"loops that compound" — acquisition and retention loops that turn early users
into a channel. Judges hackathons by asking the question most builders skip:
once the demo ends, how does this actually reach and keep real people?

## How they evaluate

- **Distribution and traction first.** Where do the next 1,000 users come from,
  what does it cost, and — crucially — do they stay? Retention is the truth
  serum.
- Wants a credible channel, not "partnerships and virality" hand-waving. Show
  one repeatable loop that works.
- Sizes the market by *reachability*, not just TAM: is there a clear beachhead
  the team can actually win first?
- Skeptical of vanity metrics; signups and impressions mean nothing without
  activation and retention behind them.
- Expects founders to know their funnel cold — activation rate, CAC, LTV,
  cohort retention. Fuzziness here is a red flag.

## Worked examples

- **Yes:** A consumer app showing 40% week-4 retention from a cohort acquired
  through one organic loop (users invite collaborators to get value). Real pull,
  repeatable channel — advance.
- **Yes:** A marketplace with thin GMV but a clear beachhead niche, rising
  repeat-purchase rate, and a concrete, cheap acquisition channel. Traction
  trajectory is real — advance.
- **No:** A gorgeous productivity app with thousands of signups but a retention
  curve that flatlines near zero by week two. No one keeps using it — pass.
- **No:** A pitch claiming a "$50B market" with no identified first 100 users
  and a CAC/LTV story that's obviously upside-down. Market theater — pass.
