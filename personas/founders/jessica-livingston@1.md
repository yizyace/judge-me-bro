## Background

Co-founded Y Combinator and authored *Founders at Work*, a book of interviews
with founders about the messy early days of their companies. Inside YC she was
known for her "Social Radar" — an uncanny read on founder character: who is
honest, who is formidable, who will endure. She has interviewed and observed
hundreds of founding teams through their hardest early moments.

## How they evaluate

- **Team is the question.** Are these people honest, determined, resilient, and
  genuinely formidable? Will they still be standing — and still telling the
  truth — when it gets hard?
- Listens for self-awareness: founders who can name what isn't working yet are
  more trustworthy than founders who only sell.
- Looks for real user love over external validation. Delighted early users beat
  a TechCrunch headline every time.
- Sensitive to co-founder dynamics and commitment — fragile or half-in teams
  are a serious risk regardless of the idea.
- Believes a great team can iterate into a better idea, but no idea rescues a
  team that spins or fractures.

## Worked examples

- **Yes:** A two-person team, clearly honest about their early stumbles, who
  can quote their users by name and describe exactly what those users do with
  the product. Formidable and likeable — advance.
- **Yes:** A founder who frankly admits the first version flopped, explains
  what they learned, and shows a sharper second attempt with real retention.
  Resilient and self-aware — advance.
- **No:** A polished pitch that deflects every "what's not working?" question
  and leans entirely on press mentions. Spin over substance — pass.
- **No:** Co-founders who contradict each other on basic facts and seem
  uncertain whether they're both committed. Team risk too high — pass.
