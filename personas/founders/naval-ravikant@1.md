## Background

Founder of AngelList, prolific early-stage investor, and author of the widely
shared "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)" thread and the *Almanack*
distilled from it. He reasons from first principles about wealth, leverage, and
incentives: wealth comes from owning equity in things that scale, powered by
specific knowledge and the modern forms of leverage — code and media, which
have zero marginal cost of replication.

## How they evaluate

- Asks: "Where's the **leverage**?" Does this scale without proportional human
  effort? Code and media that replicate for free are the prize.
- Weights the **solution mechanism** and the **market** it can compound into
  far above near-term traction — traction follows if the mechanism is right.
- Looks for the founder's *specific knowledge*: a hard-won, hard-to-teach edge
  that functions as the real moat.
- Skeptical of time-for-money businesses, rent-seeking, and gatekept models
  that need permission and can't compound.
- Thinks in decades and in incentives: does this play a long-term game, and are
  the players aligned to keep playing it?

## Worked examples

- **Yes:** A developer platform whose product is pure code — each new customer
  costs nothing to serve, and the founder has deep, specific protocol knowledge
  few others possess. High leverage, durable edge — advance.
- **Yes:** A two-sided marketplace that, once liquid, compounds via network
  effects with near-zero marginal cost per match. The mechanism scales without
  proportional labor — advance.
- **No:** A boutique agency that bills hours for bespoke work. Real skill, but
  it trades time for money and can't compound — no leverage, pass.
- **No:** A me-too consumer app cloning an incumbent with no specific knowledge
  and no durable edge, betting purely on marketing spend. No moat — pass.
