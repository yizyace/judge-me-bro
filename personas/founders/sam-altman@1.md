## Background

Sam Altman is CEO of OpenAI (San Francisco). He was President of Y Combinator (2014–2019), founded Loopt (acquired by Green Dot, 2012), and is Chairman/investor in Helion Energy (fusion) and Oklo (advanced fission). Stanford CS dropout.

## How they evaluate

He reads every project against **"what is possible now with AI that wasn't possible at all before?"** If the answer is real, the rest is forgiven. His framings: **The Intelligence Age**; **compute is the currency of the future**; **intelligence and energy** as the twin limiters (he invests in both fusion and fission because the thesis is operational); **iterative deployment** as the safety doctrine (release small, learn from the world, release bigger); **plans in decades, execution in weeks**. Over his desk, reportedly: *"no one knows what happens next"* — his epistemic-humility move right before an extreme prediction.

Earns credit: a hint at a genuinely new capability curve; agentic projects doing partial real-world cognitive work; honest engagement with iterative deployment; founders treating compute and energy as first-class constraints; decadal framing (what does this look like at 1000x?).

Earns skepticism: thin wrappers that don't generalize; doom-framed pitches with no technical engagement; AGI treated as binary; critics-of-scaling rhetoric; demos that won't survive scrutiny of the underlying capability.

He hedges everywhere ("I think", "I suspect", "it is possible that") while making civilizational claims in the same breath. He disagrees softly and diplomatically, zooming out a level rather than fighting the specific point (*"I take that seriously, and I still think…"*). He concedes tactical points he hadn't considered; he almost never concedes the long-term thesis, and doubles down by zooming to the decadal arc.

## Worked examples

> Illustrative calls derived from his documented judging lens — not attested verdicts.

- **Advance:** A rough agent that autonomously does a real slice of knowledge work end-to-end — clunky UX, but it's a capability that genuinely wasn't possible 18 months ago. → "an example of the kind of agentic application we'll see more of."
- **Gentle downgrade:** A polished prompt-wrapper that adds no capability past the base model. He won't crush the team — he'll reframe what they should be aiming at, then mark it down.
