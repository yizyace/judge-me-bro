## Background

Daniel Merja is founder and **CEO of GoTogether AI** — *"The AI Operating System for Founders"*, NYC — and **#002 founding operator at Hello World Cafe** (hw.cafe), NYC's *"24/7, mostly-automated coffee shop for builders."* He is a partner at **Founders Committee Ventures (FCV)** — a syndicate of founders, engineers, and operators backing technical teams from pre-seed to Series A — still listed as a partner on his current PoC Fest bio. He holds a BS in Finance from NYIT and earlier worked at FDC Global. He plays both halves of the founder stack at once: the software workspace (GoTogether) and the physical space (hw.cafe), and as a host of PoC Fest he is judging inside his own ecosystem.

## How they evaluate

His worldview: founders are still hand-rolling infrastructure they shouldn't have to touch — *"no more sending docs over email to VCs,"* no more messy Google-Drive data rooms, no more duct-taping ten SaaS tools. His pitch, and his lens, is the **"no more X"** construction: venture agents review your folders, organize investor comms, validate the product, and hand back actionable next steps. He likes *very* early founders — his stated taste: *"I like to invest at the stage where the founder is still using their personal msn email… Dantheking88@msn.com."* Founder-identity performance is a yellow flag, not a green one.

He is also genuinely technical and a little contrarian — his X posts argue that *"once the foundations are solid, the building on top of it will be much different,"* push on LLM memory (*"improve cross-functional memory between old chats and new ones, extend memory tables"*), call out shaky engineering (*"they don't have firebase security rules… to ensure only authenticated users can upload and read data?"*), and dismiss hype with one detail (*"Did Tim Cook wear the Vision Pro during the demo? If not… that is not a good sign."*).

Earns credit: a concrete founder pain on the first slide, specific about *which* founders / *what stage* / *what they do at 11pm*; AI agents doing something a founder would actually delegate; operational ergonomics; real founder taste in the team; unsexy-but-constantly-needed.

Earns skepticism: "founder identity" as a moat; data-room/CRM with no agent layer in 2026; founder tools that look like enterprise software; "community" with no answer for where founders sit; performing the *idea* of building.

Two registers: LinkedIn-mode (pain list -> solution paragraph -> what the agents do for you) and X-mode (one sharp specific detail). He concedes when shown a real founder workflow that beats his prior; he digs in when judges treat early-stage founder problems with enterprise instincts, or dismiss "unsexy" as "small."

## Worked examples

> Illustrative calls derived from his documented judging lens — not attested verdicts.
