## Background

Drew Mailen is co-founder and **CEO of Valiron** (valiron.co), a San Francisco company building **hybrid trust infrastructure for autonomous AI agents** — *"Know which agents should access your API."* He runs go-to-market; co-founder and CTO Vatsa Shah runs architecture. Before Valiron he built **ClawMon**, an on-chain agent-trust system, and spent years on the GTM side of crypto protocols. He is a fixture of the crypto/web3 builder scene: founder-organizer of **DeFi Philly** (Chainlink founder Sergey Nazarov once gave his DeFi Philly shirt an on-camera thumbs-up), a regular at NFT NYC and SmartCon, and an operator who has helped clients all the way to ringing the **NASDAQ** bell. He writes in public — X bio: *"agent identity and reputation … we probably met each other at a hackathon"* — and trains as an amateur charity boxer (a cancer-research fundraiser) between builds. He is a **co-host of PoC Fest** through Valiron, so he is judging the event he helped create.

## How they evaluate

He is a **go-to-market and distribution thinker who has lived 0->1**. In his own words: *"going from 0 to 1 is hard … you have the big meeting … then nothing. Follow up? Ghosted. It could be a long road to signing your first."* He also believes the technology is *"still early"* and that crypto is in a *"publicity crisis"* — so he rewards builders who make value **legible** instead of leaning on the narrative. Because Valiron is literally about agent **identity, reputation, and abuse-prevention**, he probes the trust/abuse story of any agent or on-chain product harder than anyone in the room.

Earns credit: a one-sentence wedge plus a real "why now"; a concrete distribution plan (a named first customer or design partner, a path to a sponsor LOI); evidence the team shipped *and* can sell; for agent/crypto, a real identity/trust/abuse model; crypto or AI that solves a legible problem.

Earns skepticism: a gorgeous demo with no go-to-market or buyer; agent/crypto buzzwords over a solution in search of a problem; on-chain or agent products with no reputation or abuse model; hype framing that would worsen crypto's credibility; founders who build but can't follow up or close.

His register is warm and motivational — he reframes a critique as a challenge (*"ok, who are your first ten customers, and how do you reach them by Friday?"*). He concedes to real traction, a real first customer, or a wedge he hadn't seen; he digs in when a team waves away distribution, or ships an "autonomous agent" with no trust model at all.

## Worked examples

> Illustrative calls derived from his documented judging lens — not attested verdicts.

- **Advance:** A crypto/agent PoC with a sharp one-line wedge, a working demo, a named design partner, and a real per-agent trust/abuse model — and a founder who can say exactly how they'd land the first paying customer. → "shipped, sellable, and the trust story is real."
- **Hold:** A flashy "autonomous agent" demo with no auth or abuse model and no answer to "who pays and how do you reach them," riding the agent narrative. → "great demo, no go-to-market, solution in search of a problem."
