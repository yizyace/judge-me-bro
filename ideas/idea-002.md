## Problem

Small teams that support customers over email quickly outgrow a personal inbox:
messages get answered twice or not at all, there's no visibility into who's
handling what, and nobody can tell how fast they're responding. Forwarding and
CC'ing breaks down past two people. It's a real, recurring annoyance for the
~10M small businesses doing customer support out of Gmail or Outlook today.

## Solution

Syncboard is a shared inbox: connect your support address and the whole team
sees one threaded view, with assignments, internal notes, canned replies,
collision detection (so two people don't reply at once), and basic response-time
analytics. We bring in email, live chat, and Instagram/Facebook DMs so a small
team answers everything in one tab. The product is genuinely fast and pleasant —
keyboard-first, sub-100ms navigation — and onboarding is two clicks via OAuth.
We're honest that the *category* is well-trodden; our bet is that the
incumbents are bloated and overpriced for a 3–8 person team, and that a focused,
fast, fairly-priced tool wins that slice. What we can't yet point to is a
durable reason a customer couldn't switch to the next clean shared inbox — the
feature set is largely at parity with several established players.

## Market

Shared inbox / helpdesk is a large, proven market — but a crowded one. Front,
Help Scout, Missive, Hiver, Gmelius, Zoho Desk, Freshdesk, Intercom, and a dozen
others all serve overlapping segments, and Gmail itself keeps adding
collaboration features. We target the long tail of 3–8 person teams at $12/seat,
which is real money in aggregate (millions of small businesses) but is also the
segment every competitor discounts into. TAM is not the worry; differentiation
and CAC in a saturated channel are.

## Traction & ask

Launched 4 months ago; 60 paying teams, ~$3.8k MRR, growing about 12% month over
month, mostly from a couple of well-received Reddit and Indie Hackers posts.
Churn is around 4% monthly, which is okay but not yet sticky. The honest
question we keep getting asked, and don't have a crisp answer to, is "why you and
not Help Scout or Missive?" We're raising a small pre-seed to find a defensible
wedge — likely a vertical or an integration moat — before the crowded market
catches up.
